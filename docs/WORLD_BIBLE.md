# World bible

## World structure

The island is finite, contiguous, and designed to feel open through route
choice, elevation, sightlines, loops, and recognizable silhouettes.

- A central highland provides constant broad orientation.
- An outer coastal route links the major regions.
- Interior trails offer shorter but more demanding crossings.
- Each major destination has a safe approach and a skillful approach.
- Routes reconnect; long empty dead ends are avoided.
- The world is authored region by region against one global terrain and route
  plan.

The first full-world compiler target is 1,024 by 1,024 metres. This is a
starting production constraint, not a declaration that the final game must be
exactly one square kilometre.

## Regions

### Saltwind Coast

Opening region of beaches, dunes, surf, storm debris, salt grass, and wide
skies.

Purpose:

- Establish isolation and calm
- Teach precise movement before speed
- Provide the first open gallop transition
- Establish visible tracks and the storm premise

Signature silhouettes: split sea stack, wind-bent trees, broken lookout or
weathered beacon.

### Longgrass Plain

Broad rolling grassland that expresses speed and long-distance choice.

Purpose:

- Deliver the pure gallop fantasy
- Show multiple destinations at once
- Introduce moving wildlife as navigation cues
- Create a memorable route back toward the coast and forest

Signature silhouettes: lone tree, stone ridge, distant herd-like shapes,
central highland.

### Fernwood

Dense, green woodland with tighter routes, filtered light, fallen logs, and
echoing sound.

Purpose:

- Contrast the open plain with careful control
- Test turning, braking, and readable jumps
- Make calls and environmental audio spatially meaningful
- Hide short routes that later become familiar

Signature silhouettes: giant fern grove, split cedar, ruined arch or old
boundary stones.

### River Hollow

Waterfalls, shallow fords, muddy banks, stream jumps, sheltered springs, and
resting hollows.

Purpose:

- Introduce water-edge traversal without becoming a swimming game
- Provide natural reset and save places
- Carry hoofprints and herd evidence through soft ground
- Offer a tranquil midpoint in the emotional arc

Signature silhouettes: waterfall notch, leaning stone bridge, bright spring.

### Blackstone Crown

Basalt ridges, windswept climbs, high pasture, and the final view.

Purpose:

- Test movement mastery without precision platforming
- Reframe previously visited regions from above
- Deliver the strongest call-and-response moment
- Resolve the first journey at the high pasture

Signature silhouettes: broken black ridge, summit saddle, high grass moving in
the wind.

## Vertical-slice geography

The slice contains only:

- A section of Saltwind Coast
- An opening into Longgrass Plain
- A small edge of Fernwood
- One shallow stream and jumpable obstacle
- One spring/resting hollow
- One overlook
- One wildlife encounter
- One herd trace and call response

The slice is a designed 400-500 metre journey, not a miniature version of every
future system.

## Landmark rules

- A major landmark is recognizable from a meaningful riding distance.
- Every mandatory destination has at least two independent cues.
- Landmarks shape route choice rather than merely decorating a location.
- Environmental storytelling remains readable without text logs.
- The horse can respond through horse-appropriate verbs.
- A discovery rewards attention with a view, sound, event, route, or emotional
  beat rather than inventory.

## Route rules

- Traversable terrain is planned before prop placement.
- Main paths tolerate ordinary play and camera variation.
- Optional lines reward speed control or better jumping.
- Dense props never hide the only valid route.
- Ground, vegetation, and obstacles remain legible at gallop speed.
- The player is allowed to leave trails when slopes and collision permit it.
- Mandatory discoveries remain reachable without executing a precise jump.

## Herd traces

Candidate major scenes:

1. Hoofprints preserved beside the storm beach.
2. An old resting circle in flattened long grass.
3. Hair caught on bark near a forest crossing.
4. Fresh tracks and an answering call beside the river spring.
5. The living herd revealed at the Blackstone high pasture.

The first four should each change how the player reads the island. They are not
collectibles distributed for completion percentage.

## Environmental storytelling

Useful material:

- Storm damage and regrowth
- Repeated herd paths in vegetation
- Wildlife reactions
- Water and wind carrying sound
- Abandoned structures serving as silhouettes or shelter
- Old fences or walls that create horse-scale traversal choices

Avoid:

- Written lore required for comprehension
- Machines the horse must operate
- Human NPCs giving conventional quests
- Arbitrary magical barriers
- Dense clutter that looks good when still but fails at horse speed

## Time and weather

The vertical slice uses one controlled lighting and weather state so movement,
navigation, and performance can be judged consistently. Dynamic time of day
and weather are future possibilities, not foundation requirements.
