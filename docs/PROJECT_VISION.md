# Project vision

## One-sentence pitch

You are a young wild horse separated from your herd after a storm, freely
running across an island, following natural signs and distant calls until the
unfamiliar landscape begins to feel like home.

## Player promise

The game offers the physical joy of being a horse in an open landscape:
building speed, feeling momentum, choosing a line through terrain, jumping an
obstacle, slowing to listen, and seeing a new horizon open ahead.

Exploration is not the delay between rewards. Crossing the island is itself a
reward.

## Experience pillars

### 1. Embodiment

The player should inhabit a horse rather than steer a generic third-person
avatar. Gaits, turning radius, braking, slope response, jumping, breathing,
hoof rhythm, animation, and camera response all serve that embodiment.

### 2. Freedom with physicality

Open grass invites an uninterrupted gallop. Forest and river terrain ask for
control. Speed is expressive, but it carries momentum and limits sharp turns.
The horse is powerful without being frictionless.

### 3. Curiosity without clutter

Landforms, silhouettes, trails, wildlife, tracks, wind, water, and calls draw
the player forward. Ordinary play should not depend on a minimap, floating
route line, checklist, or screen full of objective furniture.

### 4. Belonging

The emotional movement is isolation to curiosity to confidence to connection.
By learning routes and discovering herd traces, the player changes their
relationship with the island. The ending resolves the first journey without
preventing free roaming.

## Tone

- Calm, physical, atmospheric, and occasionally wondrous
- Naturalistic with restrained magical realism
- Hopeful rather than threatening
- Environmental rather than exposition-heavy
- Suitable for players who enjoy traversal, animals, nature, and discovery

The island may appear to answer the horse through echoes, birds, wind, and
distant herd calls. The horse does not cast spells or become a fantasy combat
character.

## Product shape

- Single-player browser game
- Third-person horse exploration
- One finite island with no loading screen between regions
- Compact open world designed around density, sightlines, loops, and changing
  traversal rhythm rather than raw square kilometres
- Desktop browser first; wider device support is an explicit later decision
- A first complete journey measured in tens of minutes, followed by free roam

Initial implementation dimensions are deliberately modest. The vertical slice
uses roughly 400-500 metres of playable space. A first complete island starts
around 1,024 by 1,024 metres and grows only if horse-speed playtests show that
larger scale improves the experience.

## Non-goals

- Horse survival simulator
- Combat game
- Rider or stable-management fantasy
- Crafting, loot, inventory, skill tree, or economy
- Infinite procedural world
- Precision platformer
- Human quest interactions performed by a horse
- Photorealism as a requirement
- Multiplayer in the first game

## Definition of success

The game succeeds when:

- A new player says the controller feels like a horse.
- Galloping on simple terrain is enjoyable without an objective.
- The player finds the first discovery without developer intervention or a
  mandatory minimap.
- The island is legible at horse speed.
- A player remembers places and routes rather than only remembering markers.
- The journey creates a felt transition from isolation to belonging.
